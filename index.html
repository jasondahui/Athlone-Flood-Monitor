The county sets these channel thresholds for the community just downstream of the Caltrain crossing, which includes Athlone Terrace. The street (~21 ft NAVD88) sits in a low pocket drained by the Athlone Terrace pump station, below the channel banks to the east (~27 ft). The Bay itself peaks around 9.5 ft NAVD88, so what floods the street is stormwater: hard bursts of rain that outrun the pumps, plus channel water pushing back through the outlet (slowed by the flap gate since Nov 2024).

Model Settings

The rating uses the hardest 3 hours of rain at the Caltrain gauge (measured for the past, forecast for the future). By default the future part is the consensus: each model's hardest 3 hours (HRRR × its bias factor; RRFS and NBM × 1.00 until this browser has learned their bias from 8+ storms), then the middle value. Storm-library replays use archived HRRR only. Thresholds calibrate from the storms you've labeled, within the active gate setting. Pre-gate storms still set lower bounds, since the gate can only help. A live channel level at county Monitoring, Watch or Warning raises the rating to at least BLUE, GREEN or YELLOW.

How this dashboard works: every rule it applies

This is a homemade tool, not an official warning. For official alerts, sign up for SMC Alert and follow the National Weather Service. Flood labels describe what was seen at one house on Athlone Terrace; other houses on the street may flood earlier or later.

Risk rating

  • The rating looks at the hardest 3 hours of rain from now through the end of the forecast: rain measured so far plus the HRRR forecast for the hours ahead.
  • Forecast rain is multiplied by a bias factor (default 1.25). Checked against archived HRRR for the 19 library storms with a working Caltrain gauge, the gauge's hardest 3 hours were a median 1.30× the forecast's, but individual storms ranged from 0.3× to 4×, so the factor corrects the typical miss, not the big ones.
  • The page also learns its own factor: each time it loads, it saves what HRRR forecast for 3–6 hours ahead, and once those hours have been measured it compares the two. Rain events are picked on either forecast or measured rain, so false alarms count as well as misses. The learned value (median of measured ÷ forecast hardest 3 hours) appears in Model Settings with a button to use it; it needs about 8 events before it means much.
  • That 3-hour total is compared with five thresholds, one per street level at one house on Athlone Terrace: BLUE = sidewalk (water covers the sidewalk), GREEN = yard (past the sidewalk but short of the garage door), YELLOW = garage (reaches the garage door), RED = garage + (above the garage-door base, not inside), PURPLE = structure (water inside).
  • If the channel at Caltrain is at or above the county's Monitoring (2.8 ft), Watch (4.1 ft) or Warning (5.5 ft) level right now, the rating is raised to at least BLUE, GREEN or YELLOW. Replays and the library's "Model predicts" column apply the same bump using each storm's channel peak. Both documented storms since the gate with the channel above Watch reached the garage doors.
  • If the Caltrain gauge has measured 0.40 in or more in the last hour, the rating is raised to at least YELLOW right away. The documented garage-level storms had hardest hours of 0.43 to 0.59 in, and HRRR under-forecast those bursts by about 3×, so a measured burst is a better early signal than the forecast.
  • When the forecast has rain, the risk card also says what level a 3× forecast miss would reach ("If HRRR is low…"), since that is how far off it was before the Mar 2023 and Dec 2025 floods.
  • If live data fails completely, the page shows "NO LIVE DATA" (not a past storm) and keeps retrying every 10 minutes.
  • The tide is shown for context only. The street sits at about 21 ft NAVD88 and the Bay has peaked near 9.5 ft, so the tide isn't part of the rating.

How the thresholds are set

  • The thresholds are fitted on rain only; the channel bump above is applied afterwards.
  • Only storms with a documented street outcome (a photo or a direct report) are labeled, and only labeled storms affect the thresholds.
  • Storms are split into two periods: before and after the flap gate was installed (Nov 1, 2024). The rating uses the after-gate period unless the gate setting is turned off.
  • For each level, the lower bound is the wettest labeled storm that stayed below that level (from either period, since the gate can only help). The upper bound is the driest after-gate storm that reached the level.
  • If both bounds exist, the threshold is the midpoint. If only the lower bound exists, it's 10% above it (at least +0.05 in). If neither exists, defaults are used: 0.30, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90 and 1.30 in per 3 h (sidewalk through structure). These defaults are guesses, set below the documented storms (garage at 0.95–1.07, garage + at 0.90) but not fitted to data.
  • Each level must be at least 0.05 in above the one below it (0.25 in for structure).

Which storms are in the library

  • Storm boundaries: any hour with at least 0.02 in of rain at any of the three gauges, or a channel level of 2.8 ft or more, counts as storm activity. A dry gap of more than 6 hours ends a storm; until then it shows as "in progress" and isn't used to set thresholds. Each storm's window runs from 6 hours before its first active hour to 7 hours after its last.
  • Built-in storms (Sep 2022 to Oct 2026): every storm with a channel peak of at least 4.1 ft (county Watch) or a peak 3-hour rain total of at least 0.5 in, plus any storm with a documented street outcome.
  • New storms are logged automatically when the page opens, looking back up to 14 days, if they have at least 0.25 in of total rain, at least 0.15 in in 3 hours, or a channel peak of at least 2.8 ft.

Data checks

  • Rain comes from the Caltrain gauge. If it recorded less than 40% of what a nearby gauge recorded for the same storm (and that gauge had at least 0.3 in), the nearby gauge is used instead: Redwood City first, then Palo Alto. The storm is marked with the gauge used.
  • Channel readings above 12 ft are discarded as sensor errors (the record high is 6.6 ft).
  • Rain totals come from each gauge's running total. In Feb 2026 the Caltrain gauge sent that total under a different code (PPU instead of PC); the dashboard uses the PPU value whenever a gauge has sent no PC value for more than 12 hours.
  • Live and in the library, an hour of 0.5 in or more at one gauge is treated as a sensor error and dropped when every other gauge reporting nearby had under 0.05 in in that hour and the hours either side (this caught Redwood City's +0.85 in on Nov 17, 2026). When building the built-in library, two kinds of readings were treated as sensor errors and the storm dropped: one gauge showing 0.5 in or more in an hour while the other two recorded almost nothing (under 0.05 in), which removed Dec 30, 2024; and a channel peak of 4.1 ft or more with less than 0.25 in of rain at every gauge, which removed Sep 25, 2023.
  • Recent hours use the county feed's hourly totals, which update every 10–15 minutes; older hours use the archive. Forecast rain is always a dark-blue bar and measured rain a teal bar drawn over it, so the teal fills the blue as rain falls; if the rain beats the forecast, the teal rises past it and a dark-blue outline inside the teal shows the forecast amount. Past hours keep the forecast made just before they started (from this browser's saved forecasts). Storm replays show archived HRRR for the built-in storms; note that the archive holds the very-short-range forecast (the hour or two before), which was usually closer to what fell than forecasts made hours ahead. For the rating, the current hour counts the rain so far plus the forecast for the rest of the hour. While it's raining, the page re-checks the county feed every 5 minutes.
  • Gauge readings are grouped into hour-long bins starting on the hour (rain summed, channel level = highest reading). The gauge archive is checked at most every 20 minutes (every 2 minutes with Refresh); if it doesn't answer, the last saved copy from the past 24 hours is used.
  • Labels, logged storms and settings are saved in each person's own browser. They don't sync between devices or people.

Data sources

Live data and forecasts

  • OneShoreline Flood Early Warning System (San Mateo County): Atherton Channel at Caltrain gauge (rain, channel level, county thresholds), every ~15 min.
  • Iowa Environmental Mesonet, NWS HADS archive: hourly history for gauges ACUC1 (Caltrain), RWCC1 (Redwood City) and PAAC1 (Palo Alto).
  • Open-Meteo: NOAA HRRR rain forecast (15-minute output for the next ~18 h, hourly after).
  • NOAA Tides & Currents, Redwood City 9414523: measured tide and tide predictions; SF Bay Operational Forecast System for surge.
  • National Weather Service API: active alerts for this location.

Drainage map and background

  • San Mateo County Storm Water Dataset: storm drains, outfalls, catchments.
  • County: Athlone Terrace Pump Station trash capture project, bid Q&A (48-inch influent pipe) and CEQA notice 2023120167 (station location).
  • Board of Supervisors, Athlone Way Storm Drain Rehabilitation (2002).
  • County: 18th Avenue road improvement options (2025); OneShoreline: Bayfront Canal & Atherton Channel project.
  • USGS 3DEP Elevation Point Query Service: ground elevations (ft NAVD88). OpenStreetMap contributors: street lines.
  • Flood outcomes: photos and reports from Athlone Terrace residents.
